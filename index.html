Ph.D. Candidate, Political Science — FIU

Mohammad Sayyadi

Comparative politics, political economy, and the politics of the Middle East and Iran.

I'm a comparativist with research interests in democracies and autocracies, as well as the political economy of the Global South, particularly the Middle East and Iran. My research follows two paths: the political economy of post-revolutionary states, and the mechanisms through which states perpetuate corruption, monopoly, and rent-seeking — and how these shape trust in political institutions across democracies and autocracies.

My dissertation, Putting the "Take" in Takeover: Causes and Consequences of Confiscation as a Revolutionary Practice, is chaired by Eric Lob, with Todd Makse, Eduardo Gamarra, and Zachary Levenson (Sociology) on the committee. I earned an M.A. in Political Science from Florida International University in 2024, and an M.A. from the University of Tehran, Iran, in 2014.

My methodological interests include developing original datasets and applying quantitative methods. I'm trained in both quantitative and qualitative approaches, including statistical analysis, large-N studies, process tracing, and critical discourse analysis.
